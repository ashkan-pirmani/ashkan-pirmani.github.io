Applied machine learning scientist in health research

Health data is scattered.
I work on what we can still learn when it has to stay that way.

I'm Ashkan. I came to health research from systems engineering. I work on machine learning for studies whose data sit in many places, and on a lot of what has to happen before any model can run.

Now I am testing models that adapt to each site in drug safety research, on an Innovation Mandate with Johnson & Johnson.

COVID-19 and multiple sclerosis

Four registries could not send their data, so I sent the analysis to them instead.

I designed and deployed this route. It added 3,527 records, 45.5% more than the study had without it. Most of the work was agreeing on definitions with eighteen registries, and running the analysis inside those four.

Informed MS International Federation advice · JMIR Medical Informatics, 2023

Disability progression

A model that keeps part of itself for each of 32 countries.

It predicts whose disability will get worse within two years, and adapting to each country made it better at finding them.

AUC-PR 0.535, against 0.408 without adapting · simulated federation · npj Digital Medicine, 2025

FLkit

An open guide for teams starting their first study across centers.

A clinician and an engineer each get their own first page, because they need different things first.

I lead and maintain it, since 2024 · 29 contributors

Innovation Mandate

Keeping a drug safety comparison fair when each site has only a few patients.

Comparing two medicines is only fair if the groups are alike, and small sites make that hard.

On Optum's health records and insurance claims · In progress

A project I stopped

I had built FL4E, and it worked. Then I started my next study, and I did not use it.

It was meant to save research teams from rebuilding the same foundations for every study across centers. But it was built for clicking, and my next study needed scripts. The data agreement also decided where my computing could run.

That was harder to accept than I expected. I did not build a second version; I retired it, and wrote a paper on why a working tool still went unused.

About me

I thought my PhD would start with a USB stick.

Before health, I worked on Iran Post's parcel network, deciding where the sorting hubs should go. So when I started my PhD, I expected someone to give me the data, and I would get on with the modeling. That did not happen. For almost two years, most of my work was with clinicians, engineers, the people who look after the records, and their lawyers: who held the data, what they were allowed to do with it, and whether a word meant the same thing in two registries.
